Gallop was started by three people with no funding and no sales team.

Three months in, they’d signed a Fortune 400 contract. We built the platform we wished existed, then used it ourselves to close our first enterprise deals.

The problem we started with

Most modernization projects fail the same way. A team spends months just understanding what the old system does, because the business logic lives in stored procedures, dead code, and the memory of engineers who left years ago. By the time anyone starts building the replacement, half the budget is gone and nobody fully trusts what's being rebuilt.

We'd watched this happen enough times to know the fix wasn't a better consulting process. It was giving the work to agents that could read the whole system at once, and giving humans a way to check that work at every step.

What we built

Gallop maps a legacy system in hours, not months. It plans the migration, builds the replacement, and validates that the new system behaves exactly like the old one before anyone signs off on go-live.

The work runs in five stages: Assess, Plan, Build, Review, Validate. Agents do most of the heavy lifting. They map the architecture, draft the migration plan, and write and test the code themselves. Humans stay in the loop at every stage that matters. They approve the plan, review the build, and sign off before anything goes live.

Assess → Plan → Build → Review → Validate

One recent example: a global enterprise had planned 8,000 hours for a migration. Using Gallop, it took 400.

Every stakeholder stayed aligned, and every decision along the way stayed logged and reversible.

How we’ve grown

We started with one call in month one. By month three, we'd signed a three-year contract with Haier. Five months in, we're running four active pilots with more than ten in the pipeline, and all of it is being run by three founders handling go-to-market, the platform, and every deployment themselves.

That's not a boast about being scrappy. It's the reason the platform works the way it does. We've had to be in the room for every deployment, so we've seen exactly where legacy modernization breaks, and we built Gallop to fix those specific points, not the ones a slide deck would suggest matter more.

Why humans stay in control

We don't think the answer to slow modernization is removing people from the process. It's removing the parts of the process that never needed a person in the first place, like the weeks of manual code reading, the handoffs between teams that lose context, and the late-stage surprises that stall a project for months.

Gallop's agents do the majority of the execution work, but nothing ships without a human checking it first. That's not a limitation we're working around. It's the actual design.

Where we’re headed

We're building toward a platform that keeps enterprise systems modern permanently — not as a one-time migration project but as a standing capability.

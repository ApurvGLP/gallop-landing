Gallop was started by three people with no funding and no sales team.

Three months in, they’d signed a Fortune 400 contract. We built the platform we wished existed, then used it ourselves to close our first enterprise deals.

The problem we started with

Most modernization projects fail the same way. A team spends months just understanding what the old system does, because the business logic lives in stored procedures, dead code, and the memory of engineers who left years ago. By the time anyone starts building the replacement, half the budget is gone and nobody fully trusts what's being rebuilt.

We'd watched this happen enough times to know the fix had to go deeper than a better consulting process: give the work to agents that can read the whole system at once, and give humans a way to check that work at every step.

How we’ve grown

We started with one call in month one. By month three, we'd signed a three-year contract with Riser. Five months in, we're running four active pilots with more than ten in the pipeline, and all of it is being run by three founders handling go-to-market, the platform, and every deployment themselves.

That's the reason the platform works the way it does, not a boast about being scrappy. We've had to be in the room for every deployment, so we've seen exactly where legacy modernization breaks, and we built Gallop to fix those specific points rather than the ones a slide deck would suggest matter more.

Our answer to slow modernization is to remove the parts of the process that never needed a person in the first place, like the weeks of manual code reading, the handoffs between teams that lose context, and the late-stage surprises that stall a project for months. The people stay.

Gallop's agents do the majority of the execution work, but nothing ships without a human checking it first. That check is the design, not a limitation we're working around.

We're building toward a platform that keeps enterprise systems modern permanently. A standing capability, not a one-time migration project.
